Wikipedia content gap analysis · Uganda · cycle 2026-09

Two different Ugandas exist on Wikipedia, and neither one is complete

A single-territory content gap study modelled on the Wikipedia Diversity Observatory. Across Wikipedia's 343 language editions there are 14,149 Uganda-related topics, but the editions holding the most of them describe a country of rivers and hills with almost no people in it, while the one Ugandan-language edition describes people and almost no country.

The finding

The same corpus looks completely different depending on which edition you read it in. Cebuano and Swahili hold thousands of Ugandan streams, hills and sub-counties generated in bulk by bots. Luganda holds Ugandan people, written by people.

“Places & nature” covers settlements, administrative units and physical geography; “everything else” is institutions, organisations, works and topics. Buckets are matched on Wikidata type labels, so a handful of items land in “everything else” simply for having an unusual type. Article length says the same thing: 24% of Luganda’s Uganda articles are under 2 KB, against 90% of Swahili’s, the signature of writing versus generation.

cebwiki holds more Uganda articles than English does, and 2 of them are about a Ugandan. Its Uganda corpus is bot-generated physical geography. Luganda Wikipedia, with 3,592 Uganda articles and roughly 73 active editors, is 73.3% biographies. Article counts alone cannot tell these two situations apart, which is why counting articles is not the same as measuring coverage.

The local-language gap

Luganda Wikipedia is the most Uganda-focused edition in the world, and it is still missing 10,557 of the 14,149 topics. It is also Uganda's only live Wikipedia, but not for much longer: Runyankore is approved and awaiting creation by developers; Acholi has an active Incubator test wiki with a request pending; 8 more (Rukiga, Lusoga, Lango, Ateso, Masaaba, Runyoro-Rutooro, Lugbara, Alur) have no project yet.

Who the corpus is about

Of 3,420 Ugandan biographies, 38.2% are women, roughly double Wikipedia's long-standing global figure of about 20%. This is the one dimension where Uganda's coverage is ahead rather than behind.

Luganda is at 95.9% of English coverage

For Ugandan women, Luganda Wikipedia has essentially caught up with English, and in football it is ahead. Luganda carries 44 Ugandan women footballers to English's 38, and 19 Ugandan women have a Luganda article and no English one at all. These are not placeholder stubs: across all 1,066 Luganda women's biographies the median length is 4,410 bytes, and only 12.4% fall under 2 KB against 24% for Luganda's Uganda articles overall. The pattern is consistent with a deliberate editing campaign rather than incidental growth, though this analysis measures the outcome and not the cause.

Treating these as a translation backlog would put roughly 15,000 primary schools at the top of every worklist. Uganda is well described as a database and thinly described as an encyclopedia; that is a data-quality finding, not a writing task.

How this was built. The corpus is Wikidata items about Uganda holding at least one Wikipedia sitelink, gathered by seven strategies over the Wikidata Query Service, with article sizes from the MediaWiki API and pageviews from the Wikimedia REST API.

Worklist eligibility. The corpus of 14,149 is built for recall; the worklists are built for precision. Only the 13,685 topics with a structural Wikidata link to Uganda (its country, a location inside it, Ugandan citizenship, or birth or death there) are eligible. The weaker signals earn their place in the corpus but not on a worklist: a cross-border ethnicity pulls in Kenyan athletes, and an “… in Uganda” redirect pulled in Elizabeth II. Places reached by country must also be in Uganda and only Uganda, since the Nile counts Uganda among its countries and was lending a Ugandan connection to everyone who ever drowned in it. The cost is real in the other direction too: a genuinely Ugandan topic known only by keyword, such as the Entebbe raid, sits in the corpus with is_core=0 and off the worklists. Filter ug_ccc_full_corpus.csv on that column to choose your own balance.

Other limits. Recall is a floor, not an exact count. Unlike the Diversity Observatory, this pipeline has no trained classifier, so culturally Ugandan topics with no Wikidata link to Uganda are under-counted. Pageviews are fetched for the top 2,000 topics by edition count, so demand ranking is reliable at the head of the worklists and thin in the tail. The bot/human distinction in the coverage chart is a per-edition judgement, not a per-article test. The keyword search returns a slightly different set between runs, so corpus totals move by a few dozen items cycle to cycle.

Reproduce. python src_data/run_all.py then python src_viz/build_dashboard.py. Stages checkpoint per month, so a new cycle refreshes everything. Full corpus and every table are in data/*.csv.

What to do about it. This page is the evidence. The recommendations that follow from it (prioritised, with a 90-day sequence and a baseline to measure against) are on the action plan.
