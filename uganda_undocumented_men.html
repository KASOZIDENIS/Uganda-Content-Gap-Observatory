Men running Ugandan institutions who are not on Wikimedia at all

37 of the 58 men on this roster command a division, run a police directorate, sit on the bench, lead a faith or a media house, or run a state enterprise, and have no Wikidata item and no Wikipedia article in any language. Not a thin article. Nothing.

This page does not start from Wikimedia. Every other page asks what Wikipedia is missing relative to Wikidata, which cannot answer this question: someone absent from both is invisible to a query against either. So the input is a roster of real appointments gathered from official leadership pages and the Ugandan press, covering the armed forces, the police, the bench, faith leadership, media, sport and the public service, and each name is then looked up. Every row carries its source.

How the check works

Each roster name is searched against Wikidata labels and aliases, then against English and Luganda Wikipedia. Ugandan naming order varies and Wikidata's search is a prefix match, so each name is tried several ways: in full, first and last name, and surname alone. Matching is on name tokens rather than exact strings, which is why the result is four-valued rather than a yes or no.

StatusWhat it meansWhat to do
Nothing on WikimediaNo item and no article matched the nameCreate, if the sources support it
Wikidata item, no articleAn item covers the name, no article doesWrite the article; the item is the scaffold
Similar name, needs checkingSomething with a similar name existsConfirm by hand before acting
Has an articleAn article covers the nameNothing

A status of “Nothing on Wikimedia” is a statement about a search, not proof that a person is undocumented. A name spelled differently on Wikidata, or recorded under a married or maiden name, will read as absent here. Check before creating.

Holding a big office is not the same as being notable. This is a candidate list, not a to-write list. Wikipedia needs significant coverage in independent reliable sources before an article is justified, and a leadership page on an employer's own website is not that. Wikidata's bar is lower and most of these names would clear it, which is why the two statuses are reported separately. Anyone working from this list should start from the sources, not from the row.

The roster is the part a human owns. It lives at data/roster_men_offices.csv with one row per person: name, office, organisation, sector, rank, source_url. It was seeded from official leadership pages and Ugandan press reporting and is deliberately partial: 58 names across 12 institutions is a sample, not a census, and it currently over-represents the institutions that publish their leadership. Add rows, re-run python src_data/roster_check.py, then python src_viz/export_data.py and python src_viz/build_pages.py, and this page follows.

Names and offices are as recorded by the cited source on the date it was read, and people change jobs. Nothing here is personal information: it is the name, the public office and the source that states it, which is what an editor needs and no more.
