Content Gap Analysis Wikimedia Community User Group Uganda

What Uganda's content gaps actually are, and how to close them

A measured baseline of Uganda's presence across Wikipedia's 343 language editions, and eight recommendations that follow from it. Two of them redirect work the community is most likely doing already; two more supply content to Wikipedia projects that are already in flight.

Baseline: cycle 2026-09. The numbers on this page are deliberately frozen: a plan needs a fixed starting line to measure against. Living figures are in the companion dashboard, which re-runs monthly.

Four findings that change the plan

Each of these contradicts an assumption that would otherwise shape programming. They are the reason the recommendations below look different from a standard "translate more articles" plan.

1. Counting articles gives the wrong answer

The edition with the most Uganda articles is not English. It is Cebuano, a Philippine language, with 6,765 and two of them are biographies of Ugandans. A bot generated thousands of articles about Ugandan streams, hills and sub-counties.

Luganda has 3,592 Uganda articles, of which 2,634 are about Ugandan people. Similar magnitude, completely different encyclopedia.

So what

Article counts cannot distinguish a written encyclopedia from a generated one. Any target, report or funding case built on raw counts can be satisfied by a bot without serving a single reader.

Report composition (people, places, institutions), not volume.

2. The women's translation backlog is nearly gone

Of 3,340 Ugandan women in Wikidata, 1,112 have an English article and 1,066 have a Luganda one. The English-to-Luganda backlog is 65 people.

In football and academia there is nothing left to translate; Luganda carries 44 women footballers to English's 38. Nineteen Ugandan women have a Luganda article and no English one. These are not stubs: median length 4,410 bytes, only 12% under 2 KB.

So what

Another "translate women's biographies into Luganda" campaign would spend volunteer goodwill on a solved problem.

The gap moved: 2,168 Ugandan women have no article in any language. The work is now writing from sources, not translating.

3. Luganda has people but not places

Luganda's Uganda content splits 2,634 people against 404 places. English splits 3,188 to 791; Swahili runs the other way at 638 to 3,039.

All four of Uganda's Regions (Central, Eastern, Northern, Western) have no Luganda article. Neither do the Rwenzori Mountains, Mount Elgon, Lake Kyoga or the Nile, the last of which draws over 400,000 English readings every six months.

So what

The gap in Luganda is not a general shortage. It is a specific, nameable shortage of geography and institutions, in the language spoken where that geography is.

This is a well-defined campaign with a finishable target list.

4. Uganda is over-described as data, under-described as encyclopedia

Wikidata holds 68,587 items whose country is Uganda. 58,240 of them (85%) have no article in any language. They are bulk-imported registries: roughly 15,000 primary schools, 24,000 settlements, 4,800 parishes.

Meanwhile Luganda is still Uganda's only live Wikipedia, though not for much longer. Runyankore is approved and awaiting creation; Acholi is in Incubator with a request pending; eight further languages have no project at all.

So what

Part of Uganda's deepest gap is not fixable by editing: data without prose, and languages without a project.

But the language work is no longer purely advocacy. Two projects are already in flight and need content, now. That is work this analysis can supply directly.

Recommendations

Ordered by priority. Each names the file that contains the actual list, so none of this requires re-deriving the analysis.

P1

Clear the women's translation backlog in one session

It is 65 people. That is finishable in a single editathon, and it converts a long-running programme into a completed one you can report as closed.

Start with the most-read: Sheila Atim (95,196 readings in six months), Salma Lakhani (20,463), Judith Ayaa, Neema Iyer. Law is the one field where English is meaningfully ahead: 25 articles to Luganda's 17.

Move women's programming from translating to writing

P2

Run a "Places of Uganda" campaign in Luganda

P2

Report composition, and make it the metric everyone quotes

Replace "how many Uganda articles does X have" with "how many are about people, places, institutions". Publish the dashboard each month so the User Group, the Foundation and partners are all reading the same number.

This is the cheapest recommendation here and it protects every other one: it makes bot-inflated progress visible instead of flattering.

P1

Get Runyankore Wikipedia a day-one content plan before it launches

Runyankore is already approved by the Language Committee and waiting on developers to create it (task T429189). It does not need advocacy. It needs content, and it needs it ready for the day the wiki appears.

A new Wikipedia's first few hundred articles decide whether it reads as a real encyclopedia or an empty shell. A 255-topic starter list is already generated: 150 Uganda-wide essentials plus 105 topics from the Ankole sub-region where the language is actually spoken (Mbarara City, Mbarara University of Science and Technology, Ntungamo, Ntare School, Lake Mburo, Bushenyi).

That Ankole half matters most. It is the content no other edition has any reason to write, and it is what makes a Runyankore Wikipedia worth reading rather than a thinner copy of English.

P3

Open an advocacy track for the eight languages with no project

Rukiga, Lusoga, Lango, Ateso, Masaaba, Runyoro-Rutooro, Lugbara and Alur have no test project at all. No editing campaign can reach them; this needs Incubator test wikis, Language Committee engagement and university or cultural-institution partners.

P3

Run a Wikidata quality drive alongside the writing

The analysis inherits Wikidata's errors: it surfaced an American actress recorded as a Ugandan citizen, and a Harry Potter school filed as a Ugandan administrative unit. Cleanup improves the encyclopedia and sharpens every future measurement at the same time.

The 58,240 article-less school and village records are not waste: they are a foundation for later articles and for reuse outside Wikipedia. They just should never be counted as coverage.

DON'T

Do not bulk-generate stub articles to raise the count

It is tempting: Uganda has 58,240 ready-made Wikidata records, and a bot could turn them into Luganda articles overnight. Article counts would jump and Luganda would leap up every ranking.

Cebuano is the proof of what that produces. The largest Uganda encyclopedia in the world, and a reader looking for a Ugandan person finds two. It would also wreck the one thing Luganda currently does better than English: a genuinely written, human encyclopedia with a 12% stub rate on its women's biographies.

First 90 days

A sequence that produces a measurable delta by the end of the quarter, and one completed programme to report.

  1. Weeks 1-2

    Publish the baseline and agree the metric

    Circulate the dashboard. Get the User Group to adopt composition rather than article count as the reported measure, before any campaign starts and can be judged by the old one.

  2. Weeks 3-4

    Translation sprint: clear all 65

    One editathon, one finishable list. Fast visible win, and it retires the translation programme cleanly rather than letting it drift.

  3. Weeks 5-8

    First creation cohort: women in politics and academia

    423 candidates between the two fields, the best-documented first. Pair with a library or newsroom for sourcing, since this is the step where translation skills alone are not enough.

  4. Weeks 9-12

    Places of Uganda, then re-measure

    Geography sprint on the ranked list, starting with the Nile and the four Regions. Then re-run the pipeline and publish the delta against this baseline: that comparison is the grant report.

  5. In parallel, throughout

    Runyankore pre-launch sprint

    This one is not sequenced by us: it is sequenced by the developer queue, and the wiki could be created at any point. Run the 255-topic starter list as a standing workstream with Ankole-region contributors so that whenever creation lands, the wiki opens with an encyclopedia rather than a main page.

What this analysis cannot tell you

Stating the limits is part of the job. Every one of these is a real constraint on how far the recommendations above can be pushed.

  • Recall is a floor, not a true count. The Wikipedia Diversity Observatory uses a machine-learning classifier trained on hand-labelled data to catch culturally Ugandan topics with no formal Wikidata link to Uganda. This pipeline uses seven explicit rules instead, so the real corpus is somewhat larger than 14,149.
  • It measures content, not people. Nothing here explains why gaps exist, who is editing, or whether new editors stay. Luganda's 73 active editors are the binding constraint on every recommendation, and this analysis has nothing to say about growing that number.
  • Data quality is inherited. Wrong citizenship records and odd classifications flow straight through from Wikidata into the worklists. Expect to discard some rows by hand.
  • The bot/human distinction is a judgement per edition, inferred from composition and stub rates: strong evidence, not a per-article test.
  • Reader demand is English-shaped. Priority ranking uses English Wikipedia pageviews because Luganda's traffic is too small to rank on. What English readers want is a proxy for what Luganda readers want, and an imperfect one.
  • The campaign attribution is inferred. Luganda's parity on women's biographies looks like organised effort, but this measures the outcome and not the cause. Confirm with the people who ran it.
  • Incubator content is invisible to this analysis. Test wikis live at incubator.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wp/<code> and are not Wikidata sitelinks, so whatever Runyankore and Acholi have already written cannot be measured here. Their starter lists are proposals, not gap measurements: check them against the test wikis before assigning work, or some of it will already be done.

Method. Uganda's content corpus is Wikidata items about Uganda holding at least one Wikipedia sitelink, gathered by seven retrieval strategies over the Wikidata Query Service, with article sizes from the MediaWiki API and pageviews from the Wikimedia REST API. Worklists are gated on a structural link to Uganda rather than a keyword match. Modelled on the Wikipedia Diversity Observatory by Marc Miquel and David Laniado, rebuilt to run without Wikimedia Cloud access.

Everything is reproducible. python src_data/run_all.py rebuilds all 24 datasets; python src_viz/build_dashboard.py rebuilds the dashboard. Stages checkpoint per calendar month. Source data from Wikidata (CC0) and Wikipedia (CC BY-SA).
